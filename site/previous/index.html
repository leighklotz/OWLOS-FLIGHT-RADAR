OWLOS Flight Radar

The planes around your home on an ESP32 board with a screen: each one a silhouette turned the way it flies, coloured by its height, with a trail behind it. Tap one for its callsign, type, height and speed. It takes the planes from the free community ADS-B networks, or from Flightradar24 if you have a key. Flash it below, then send it your WiFi and your home point over the same USB cable.

What it does and what 0.6 added, in about a minute. The planes are a demo sky on the PC simulator.

Try it in your browser: a demo sky, or your own board's planes over the USB cable.

1 / FLASH THE BOARD

The newest firmware: @VERSION@. What changed in each version.

  1. Use Chrome or Edge on a computer, a Chromebook too. Flashing goes over Web Serial, phones and Firefox can't do it.
  2. Plug the board in with a USB cable that carries data (some only charge).
  3. Press CONNECT & FLASH of your board, pick its port and say yes to erase: whatever was on the board is replaced.
  4. No port in the list? On Windows the CH340 driver may be missing. Won't connect? Hold BOOT while you plug it in.
  5. Boards with touch ask you once to press 5 crosses, so the touch knows where the screen is.
  6. A 2.8" board with the picture mirrored or in wrong colours: press BOOT in the first 8 seconds after power on. How, under the boards.
  7. Until it has WiFi the board flies a made-up DEMO sky and says so in big letters. The 2.8" and 3.5" CYD take the WiFi right there: SET UP WIFI, tap your network, type its password (step 3 shows where). Or send it from this page, step 2.
  8. Close the flasher's window when it's done: the port is free again for step 2.
  9. An update is the same button, with no to erase: the board keeps its WiFi, home, watch list and the rest. A board on WiFi finds out by itself when a new version is here: a small NEW VERSION note on its screen opens ABOUT, which has a code to scan for this page.

2.8" BOARDS: WRONG PICTURE? PRESS BOOT

The 2.8" CYD is sold with three different screen chips that look the same from outside, and the board can't tell which one it has. It starts as v2, the one most boards have. Picture mirrored, upside down, in wrong colours or like a negative: your screen is another one, and the BOOT button switches it.

  1. Power the board on, or press RST and let go.
  2. Within 8 seconds press BOOT once, briefly. A card on the screen says which one is on and shows the time left. On the 5 crosses page right after flashing BOOT works at any time.
  3. Every press starts the screen as the next one: v2, then v1, then v3, and round again. Press till the picture looks right.
  4. That's all: the board keeps it, through a reset and a power cut.

The ES3C28P has the same three to go through, it starts as v2 too. The NM-CYD-C5 has two: RGB first, then BGR. The CrowPanel Advance has the same two the other way round, BGR first. The 5" boards have them too, RGB first. Press BOOT if red and blue look swapped.

Missed the 8 seconds? Press RST and start again. Don't hold BOOT while you press RST: that wakes the flasher and the screen stays dark.

2 / SEND IT YOUR SETTINGS

WIFI

The ESP32 sees 2.4 GHz networks only. A router with one name for both bands works. Leave both empty and the board keeps the WiFi it has, for one set on its own screen. The board remembers five networks and joins whichever it finds, a sixth takes the oldest one's place.

HOME

The middle of the scope, kept on the board as you type it. Till you send one the board sits in Trenčín, Slovakia; leave the numbers empty and it keeps the home it has. The plane network only hears it rounded to about a kilometre: enough to say where to look, not where you live. FIND looks a town you type up on OpenStreetMap and puts its point in the numbers; with no town typed, it names the nearest one to the numbers, asked with the same rounded point. Leave the town and the country empty and the board asks for them itself.

WHERE THE PLANES COME FROM
THE SCREEN

Planes to follow: up to 8 callsigns, registrations (with their dash) or hex addresses. The board says when one comes in range and picks it, and when one takes off or lands anywhere: it asks the free networks about one of them every 15 seconds. Left empty, the board keeps its list; none empties it.

The LED: red blinking fast for an emergency squawk round home, green when a watched plane comes in range, blue when one takes off, cyan when one lands, white for any plane in the air near home, amber for no data. Each one on or off, and the test, are on the board: SET, the LED tab. No red in the test: the board is one whose red is on the other pin, change it here or with RED ON in the same tab.

Optional. Boards with touch change these on the screen itself (the look's button over the scope, and SET). The 3.5" RPi LCD has no touch: set them here. The map comes from OpenStreetMap: the board downloads the few pictures of it round home that the scope shows and keeps them in its flash, so a range it has shown once comes back at once, even without WiFi.

The password and the key go from this page into the board over the USB cable and nowhere else. The page keeps nothing. The board never shows them and never sends them back: it only says whether a key is stored. You can change the screen's own settings later on the board itself.
